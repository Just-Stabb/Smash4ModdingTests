# Mods, desync and matchmaking isolation (Smash 4 Wii U modpack)

Research date: 2026-10-23. Web access was partial: ssbwiki.com, GitHub raw files and repos were readable; GameFAQs, the Pretendo forum and dolphin-emu.org were behind bot challenges, so claims from them come from search-result summaries and are marked as such. Code was read directly from these repos: PretendoNetwork/super-smash-bros-wiiu (commit c1a0577, 2026-10-14), PretendoNetwork/nex-protocols-common-go (commit b5bf44f, 2026-10-21), kinnay/NintendoClients (master + wiki), dolphin-emu/dolphin (master), project-slippi/slippi-launcher (main).

## (a) How Smash 4 Wii U behaved online with mods; Nintendo bans; integrity checks; Smash 4 "Legacy"

### Takeaway
Community sources agree that cosmetic-only Sm4sh mods (music, textures, recolours) were used online without obvious problems, while moveset, physics or model changes desynced, and the modded player was the one disconnected. No source shows a Nintendo ban wave against Sm4sh modders or a file-integrity check in Smash 4 matchmaking. The only "check" was the game version: online required the current patch. I could not find any source for an online-capable "Smash 4 Legacy" mod.

### Cited Findings
- SmashWiki's general rule: "Players using identical hacks generally do not cause a desynch, but any hack that not all players share carries a risk of desynching. Some categories of hacks, such as textures, carry very little risk of desynching, while things such as model hacks are very likely, and moveset and physics hacks are nearly guaranteed to desynch." — [SmashWiki: Online desynchronization](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization) (wiki text, no citation for this sentence)
- SmashWiki on Smash 4 specifically: "In Super Smash Bros. for Wii U and Ultimate, the player using a hack is instantly disconnected from the match upon using a move that could desynchronize the game." The wiki gives no source and does not say how detection works. It may be a desync detector rather than a hack detector. — [SmashWiki: Online desynchronization](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- How Smash detects a desync, per SmashWiki: the games mostly send information about the actions performed, plus "loading" messages. When one side loads something (example: a Final Smash after a Smash Ball) that the other side did not, the unexpected "loading successful" message is treated as an error. "After around 10 seconds, both ends acknowledge that a desynch has occured, and all players are disconnected." — [SmashWiki: Online desynchronization](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- SmashWiki also says Smash uses delay-based netcode: "the game pausing on both ends until a player's given input is communicated to and confirmed on the opponent's side". — [SmashWiki: Online desynchronization](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- Version gating precedent: in Smash 4 and Ultimate, gameplay-changing patches broke replays, so Nintendo made replays from earlier versions unviewable after an update. Replays are input recordings, and "any change to the characters, stage, or items involved in a match will make desyncs inevitable". — [SmashWiki: Online desynchronization (Replay section)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- Anecdote (GBAtemp, as summarised by search): with a character-replacement mod, "the other person saw the character it actually was and it caused a desync when they tried to launch a different fighter." This fits the model where only inputs and character IDs are sent and each console uses its own local files. — [GBAtemp: Sm4shExplorer thread](https://gbatemp.net/threads/sm4shexplorer-wii-u-only-for-now.416118/) (page not directly readable, from a search summary)
- Anecdote (GameFAQs, from a search summary; page blocked): a pure reskin is "safe" because the opponent's console "still registers the character as the original fighter". A model swap with a different moveset breaks because the two sides "don't have the same hitboxes or damage values". — [GameFAQs board thread 73769135](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/633202-super-smash-bros-for-wii-u/73769135)
- Anecdote on bans (GameFAQs, from a search summary): mods "should not get you banned … as long as you keep it offline". Music "shouldn't be an issue since that's something that isn't shared between players online". One user claimed console bricking for going online with moveset edits, which is unsupported. — [GameFAQs: Will I get banned for using music mods?](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/633202-super-smash-bros-for-wii-u/73750530); [GameFAQs Q&A: Is modding Sm4sh safe?](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/wii-u/633202-super-smash-bros-for-wii-u/answers/444439-is-modding-sm4sh-safe)
- Googie's Sm4sh Mod Pack (GBAtemp, 2016): the author believed it "won't get you banned from online (as far as I know)" and warned that modding is against Nintendo's rules. The pack added content instead of replacing base/DLC content. Support was dropped on 9 Dec 2016. — [GBAtemp: Googie's Sm4sh Mod Pack](https://gbatemp.net/threads/googies-sm4sh-mod-pack.432557/) (from a search summary)
- Tooling of the era: Sm4shExplorer (early 2016) rebuilt packed files, which were loaded via SDCafiine. — [GBAtemp: Sm4shExplorer](https://gbatemp.net/threads/sm4shexplorer-wii-u-only-for-now.416118/); [GBAtemp: apply 2 mods](https://gbatemp.net/threads/ssb4-apply-2-mods-at-the-same-time.440318/)
- Balance mods existed. "Sm4sh Remix" (2017) was described as the first dedicated Sm4sh balance mod. I found no description of its online behaviour. — [ssbworld: Sm4sh Remix](https://ssbworld.com/blog/172/presenting-the-first-dedicated-sm4sh-balance-mod--sm4sh-remix)
- Current online status: official Smash 4 online ended on 8 Apr 2024 with the Wii U/3DS Nintendo Network shutdown. Pretendo's Smash for Wii U left beta and became public in Jan 2025. — [Wikipedia: Super Smash Bros. for 3DS and Wii U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Smash_Bros._for_Nintendo_3DS_and_Wii_U); [Wikipedia: Pretendo Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretendo_Network)
- Unofficial Pretendo wiki status for Smash for Wii U: "Public". Miiverse stage, Conquest and Spectating do not work. "All game modes are functional, but the server can't create more rooms for now", and a communication error means the room is full. — [Unofficial Pretendo wiki: Game support status](https://wiki.pretendo.zip/game-support-status) (read 2026-10-23)
- Cemu can play Smash for Wii U online through Pretendo, using online files dumped from a real Wii U. — [Cemu guide: Online play](https://cemu.cfw.guide/online-play.html)

### Inferences
- Smash 4's online model sends inputs plus a small amount of event/load sync. Each console simulates the match from its own local data and checks nothing ahead of time. A modded player therefore matched freely with vanilla players. Cosmetic-only differences stayed invisible, and gameplay differences diverged until the built-in desync detector dropped the match. This is the same lockstep failure mode the modpack will hit.
- "The player using a hack is disconnected" is better read as "the desync detector fires when the simulations diverge". Nothing points to signature checks on fighter files.

### Gaps
- "Smash 4 Legacy" / "Legacy mod for Smash 4": no source found. ("Legacy XP" and "Legacy TE" are Brawl/Project M-engine mods, but I did not confirm this with a fetched source.) I found no evidence of a Sm4sh-wide balance mod with its own online infrastructure.
- No primary source for any Nintendo action (bans, console bans) against Sm4sh mod users. Only forum opinion.
- No source on Cemu graphic packs and Smash 4 online (for example, whether 60FPS/speed packs desync). See inference in (b).
- I could not read the GameFAQs or GBAtemp threads directly, so poster names and dates are unverified.

## (b) Which changes are desync-safe vs desync-causing in lockstep/delay-based play

### Takeaway
Under lockstep/delay netcode (and under rollback too), any data or code that feeds the deterministic simulation must be bit-identical on all peers. Anything that only affects rendering or audio output can differ. For Smash 4 that splits the modpack into (1) safe: textures, most UI art, music and sounds, effects that don't spawn gameplay objects; (2) unsafe: fighter_param, ACMD (game scripts), MSC, hitboxes and collision (LVD), stage logic, items, new fighter or weapon kinds, RNG consumers, and any RPX code cave that runs inside the game step. Model swaps are a grey zone because bones drive hitboxes and hurtboxes.

### Cited Findings
- Risk tiers per SmashWiki: textures carry "very little risk", models are "very likely" to desync, movesets and physics are "nearly guaranteed" to. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- A loaded-asset mismatch (one side loads a Final Smash the other doesn't) is itself detected as a desync after about 10 s. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- Rollback is no cure. Slippi's rollback "begins the correct animation and skips ahead to its intended frame", which "can cause desyncs if physics bones have hurtboxes in them, such as Fox's tail". So bone and animation data are simulation-relevant. — [SmashWiki (cites a Slippi talk)](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- Gameplay patches desync input-replay systems. Smash 4/Ultimate replays break across versions for exactly this reason. — [SmashWiki](https://www.ssbwiki.com/Online_desynchronization)
- Client-side timing that isn't synchronised creates unfairness rather than desync. HDR's online latency slider changes only the local input delay. The co-author explains that Ultimate does not synchronise input latency between players, so a modded player can have 3 fewer frames of delay than a vanilla one ("the input slider value replaces the 4f provided by ultimate"). — [blu-dev: Latency Slider Explanation (gist)](https://gist.github.com/blu-dev/e227d57a9e745fd8768bc3d41c8ea6b4)
- Dolphin's sync identifier hashes everything that affects netplay/TAS sync. It is "intended to be … Identical for discs with no differences that affect netplay/TAS sync [and] Different for discs with differences that affect netplay/TAS sync". For mod-descriptor (Riivolution) games it mixes in the patch XML and every enabled option. — [Dolphin SyncIdentifier.h](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/Core/SyncIdentifier.h); [Dolphin GameFile.cpp GetSyncHash](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/UICommon/GameFile.cpp)
- Minecraft Forge's handshake rejects on network-channel mismatch. Client-only mods (minimaps, shaders) register no channel and cannot cause rejection. This is the same "simulation vs presentation" split, enforced at connect time. — [GameServerKings: mismatched mod channel list](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/minecraft/error-mismatched-mod-channels/); [Aternos: Mismatched mods](https://support.aternos.org/hc/en-us/articles/11901646576541-Mismatched-mods-Minecraft-Java-Edition)

### Inferences
Classification for this modpack (inference from the sources above plus how Smash 4 works):
- **Safe to differ (presentation only):** music/BGM and sound banks (as long as no sound call has gameplay side effects), textures (.nut), most UI (CSS/portraits, damage-panel art), Cemu resolution/shader graphic packs, and effect (ptcl) visuals that don't spawn hitbox-carrying objects.
- **Must match exactly:** fighter_param and other param files, ACMD game scripts (hitboxes, timers, user variables), MSC scripts (`*.mscsb`), motion data whose bones are used by hitboxes or hurtboxes (so new or retargeted animations count), stage LVD collision and stage logic (moving collision, breakable blocks, mobs), item tables, new fighter/weapon kinds and their slots (0x42 Sephiroth, 0x43 Incineroar), and every RPX code cave that runs during the match (new ACMD commands, tracking, roll-off, Stick Smash handling, Training-mode skip if it touches match setup). Effect and sound scripts that branch on gameplay state are probably safe, but expression scripts that set flags would not be.
- **RNG:** anything that draws from the shared gameplay RNG (random monster spawns per match, biome choice, day/night start) must be identical and must draw the same number of times. A per-console RNG used only for visuals is safe only if it is a separate generator. "Random monsters per match" and "biome selection by controller state at stage load" are high-risk. The biome choice must come from synchronised data (match settings/inputs), not local controller state, or each peer will load a different stage folder. That would desync (and also trigger the loaded-asset mismatch).
- **Frame-timing changes:** a Cemu or graphic-pack hack that changes simulation steps per frame, or a code cave that changes hitlag or speed only on one side, desyncs. A pure display-rate change does not, as long as the game still steps once per sync tick (needs testing on Cemu).
- **New fighter kinds the other side lacks:** the peer will either crash or substitute something else (compare the GBAtemp anecdote of the opponent seeing "the character it actually was"). Either way the match is lost. A vanilla peer must never be paired with a modded one.
- **Partial-match tolerance is not possible:** with a growing pack, "mostly the same version" is equivalent to "different". Isolation and version checks have to be exact (hash of the whole gameplay set).

### Gaps
- No source measured Smash 4 Wii U's exact desync detection (checksum of state vs load events only). SmashWiki is the only description.
- Not verified whether Smash 4 uses one gameplay RNG shared across effects and gameplay. This needs reverse engineering in the RPX.
- No source on Cemu frame-timing graphic packs and online desync.

## (c) How other communities keep everyone on identical builds and isolate modded players

### Takeaway
Successful projects combine three things: (1) one canonical build distributed by a launcher or updater; (2) a connect-time identity check (hash or version) that refuses mismatches; (3) structural isolation from vanilla players (separate matchmaking pool, disabled public matchmaking, or a different game identity). Slippi and Dolphin do (2) with hashes. HDR does (1) and (3) by disabling Quickplay and relying on arenas. Forge does (2) per network channel. RetroArch requires byte-identical content.

### Cited Findings
- **Slippi Launcher** computes the SHA-1 of the user's ISO. It accepts a fixed list of known-good hashes (NTSC-U 1.02 plain/NKIT/GCZ/CISO/scrubbed variants, NTSC-J 1.02) and explicitly flags known modded or other builds as invalid ("20XX 3.02", "20XX 4.07++", "Training Mode v1.1", PAL, 1.00/1.01, demo). It does a fast pre-check of the revision byte at offset 7 (must be 2), and any hash not in the list returns `UNKNOWN`. — [slippi-launcher src/main/verify_iso.ts](https://github.com/project-slippi/slippi-launcher/blob/main/src/main/verify_iso.ts)
- Slippi release notes (as summarised by search): the launcher "added validation to ISO to ensure NTSC 1.02 is used", and later "a scrubbed ISO will now show up as valid". — [slippi-launcher releases](https://github.com/project-slippi/slippi-launcher/releases?page=7)
- **Dolphin netplay** sends a `SyncIdentifier` {dol_elf_size, game_id, revision, disc_number, is_datel, 20-byte sync_hash} when the host selects a game. Each client compares it and reports `SameGame`, `DifferentHash`, `DifferentDiscNumber`, `DifferentRevision`, `DifferentRegion` or `DifferentGame`. The server also has a `ComputeGameDigest` message (the full "checksum" tab), syncs save data (`SyncSaveData`) and syncs Gecko/AR codes (`SyncCodes`) before start. — [Dolphin SyncIdentifier.h](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/Core/SyncIdentifier.h); [Dolphin GameFile.cpp](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/UICommon/GameFile.cpp); [Dolphin NetPlayServer.cpp](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/Core/NetPlayServer.cpp)
- Dolphin also refuses mismatched emulator versions: "Dolphin will not allow users to use mismatched versions for Netplay, to prevent desyncs". The guide's checksum tab verifies that everyone's game "(and SD card, if applicable…)" matches. This matters for Project+, whose content lives on a virtual SD card. — [Dolphin Netplay Guide](https://dolphin-emu.org/docs/guides/netplay-guide/) (from a search summary; page behind a bot challenge); NetPlayServer.cpp shows `ConnectionError::VersionMismatch` — [source](https://github.com/dolphin-emu/dolphin/blob/master/Source/Core/Core/NetPlayServer.cpp)
- Project+/PM community troubleshooting: desyncs from a wrong ISO dump, mismatched Gecko codes and different Dolphin revisions. "builds will not sync with each other". — [SmashLadder Desync Troubleshooting Guide](https://www.smashladder.com/guides/view/26pv/desync-troubleshooting-guide); [Smashboards: Need help with Desync on P+](https://smashboards.com/threads/need-help-with-desync-on-p.487043/)
- **HDR (HewDraw Remix, Ultimate):** per its co-author, "HDR disables the quickplay button, meaning you cannot play quickplay at all" and "HDR immediately desyncs if the other player in a battle arena does not have HDR installed". The latency slider was built into the HDR plugin "so it could not be disabled or enabled directly". Together these ensure only HDR users play HDR users. — [blu-dev gist](https://gist.github.com/blu-dev/e227d57a9e745fd8768bc3d41c8ea6b4) (gist undated; HDR announced the slider in late Nov 2022: [HDR on X](https://x.com/hewdrawremix/status/1597744718778990593))
- HDR distribution: stable releases on GitHub (HDR-Releases), nightlies selectable "by switching to it in the HDR launcher options menu". There is an on-boot HDR launcher with a play button, mods are toggled in an in-game mod manager, and holding L at boot disables all mods. The launcher has a "Play Vanilla" button request. — [HDR-Releases README](https://github.com/HDR-Development/HDR-Releases); [HewDraw-Remix issue #2615](https://github.com/HDR-Development/HewDraw-Remix/issues/2615)
- HDR README: the full overhaul is distributed as one package, including permitted third-party assets and plugins. — [HewDraw-Remix README](https://github.com/HDR-Development/HewDraw-Remix)
- Community HDR netplay guide: setting the buffer from ping/8, default 4. — [Outcaster's Notebook: HDR Netplay Guide](https://theoutcastersnotebook.com/post/hdr-netplay-guide/)
- **RetroArch / Smash Remix:** netplay requires the same RetroArch version, core version and the exact same content. Guides say a byte-for-byte identical ROM is needed and checksum mismatch is the most common problem. Smash Remix ships as a patch applied to the base ROM, so every player produces the same patched ROM. — [libretro netplay FAQ](https://github.com/libretro/docs/blob/master/docs/guides/netplay-faq.md); [Scribd: Smash Remix LEAME](https://es.scribd.com/document/804775724/LEAME) (from a search summary)
- **Minecraft Forge:** the handshake exchanges a mod list and a channel list. Rejection ("mismatched mod channel list") happens on channel or version mismatch. Sources disagree on whether the mod list alone can reject. — [GameServerKings](https://www.gameserverkings.com/knowledge-base/minecraft/error-mismatched-mod-channels/); [wiki.vg: Forge Handshake](https://wiki.vg/User:Pokechu22/Forge); [Aternos forum log](https://board.aternos.org/thread/60802-can-t-connect/)

### Inferences
- The strongest and simplest pattern is Dolphin's: one identity tuple, including a hash over everything that affects sync, compared before the match. Mod-descriptor patches are mixed into the hash, so "base + patch set" is identified exactly. A Smash 4 pack can copy this by hashing the gameplay-relevant files plus the RPX patch list into one manifest hash, and refusing play when the hashes differ.
- Slippi shows a whitelist model (known-good hashes, explicit blacklist of known mods, `UNKNOWN` otherwise). HDR shows the "isolation by construction" model: disable public matchmaking, play in arenas/friends, and accept that a mismatch desyncs instantly.

### Gaps
- ARCropolis: its README contains nothing on online play or mod-list hashing. I found no source that ARCropolis hashes mod lists for online play.
- Rivals of Aether Workshop online (version/checksum enforcement for workshop characters): not researched (tool budget).
- Smash Remix's own netplay docs (official hash list or required build) not fetched. Only general RetroArch requirements were found.
- Project+ launcher internals (auto-update, SD hash) not found. Only Dolphin's general SD checksum feature.

## (d) Pretendo policy, NEX matchmaking isolation levers, and implications for an RPX-patching, growing modpack

### Takeaway
On Pretendo, cheating in public matches is bannable, and modded or cheat play is tolerated only in private matches where everyone has agreed. A modpack that changes gameplay therefore must not reach random vanilla players. The Smash for Wii U server (NEX 3.6, access key `2869ba38`) uses Pretendo's common matchmaking, which filters sessions by exact or ranged `game_mode`, `attribs[]` (except index 1), `matchmake_system_type`, min/max participants and `codeword`. An RPX patch that writes a value vanilla never uses into a field vanilla always searches on would put modded players in their own pool with no server changes. The safest design combines that with a modpack version hash and Friends/arena-only play.

### Cited Findings
- Pretendo FAQ (from a search summary; forum page behind a bot challenge): cheats are acceptable only "in private matches where everyone knows cheats are allowed". Gaining an unfair advantage or disrupting non-consenting players, as in public matches, is bannable. Pretendo applies account and console bans on Wii U and 3DS, uses measures that defeat "traditional" ban evasion, and does not inherit Nintendo's bans. — [Pretendo forum FAQ](https://forum.pretendo.network/t/frequently-asked-questions-regarding-pretendo-network-and-pretendo-forums/4339)
- A community guide by a Pretendo developer (not official policy): the team "takes online cheating quite seriously" and wants long videos as evidence. — [hackmd: pn cheater reports](https://hackmd.io/@quarky/pn-cheater-reports)
- Pretendo's Smash Wii U server: authentication and secure servers use NEX library version 3.6.0 and access key `2869ba38`. Registered protocols are MatchMaking, MatchMakingExt, the Smash-specific MatchmakeExtension (tournament/community competition methods, mostly stubbed or commented out) and MatchmakeReferee. DataStore handles replays, profiles and the fighting-power chart. — [PretendoNetwork/super-smash-bros-wiiu nex/secure.go, nex/authentication.go, nex/register_common_secure_server_protocols.go](https://github.com/PretendoNetwork/super-smash-bros-wiiu)
- Pretendo common matchmaking (`FindMatchmakeSessionBySearchCriteria`) builds SQL filters: `ms.refer_gid=$1 AND ms.codeword=$2 AND array_length(ms.attribs,1)=$3`. Each non-empty `attribs[i]` criterion (except index 1, "reserved for the selection method") becomes an exact match or a `BETWEEN min AND max` range. The same applies to `game_mode`, `matchmake_system_type` and min/max participants. Selection orders by `RANDOM()` or by closeness to `attribs[1]`. — [nex-protocols-common-go matchmake-extension/database/find_matchmake_session_by_search_criteria.go](https://github.com/PretendoNetwork/nex-protocols-common-go/blob/main/matchmake-extension/database/find_matchmake_session_by_search_criteria.go)
- NEX MatchmakeSession fields (from NEX 2.x onward): `m_GameMode` (u32), `m_Attribs` (List<u32>), `m_OpenParticipation`, `m_MatchmakeSystemType`, `m_ApplicationBuffer`, `m_ParticipationCount`. 3.5 adds `m_ProgressScore`, `m_SessionKey`, `m_Option0`. 3.6 adds `m_MatchmakeParam` and `m_StartedTime`. `m_Codeword` only arrives in NEX 4.0. `MatchmakeSessionSearchCriteria` carries `m_Attribs` (List<String>), `m_GameMode`, `m_MinParticipants`, `m_MaxParticipants`, `m_MatchmakeSystemType` (all strings, so ranges are possible), `m_VacantOnly`, `m_ExcludeLocked`, `m_ExcludeNonHostPid`, `m_SelectionMethod`, plus `m_VacantParticipants` (3.5) and `m_MatchmakeParam` (3.6). — [NintendoClients wiki: Match Making Types](https://github.com/kinnay/NintendoClients/wiki/Match-Making-Types); [NintendoClients nintendo/nex/matchmaking.py](https://github.com/kinnay/NintendoClients/blob/master/nintendo/nex/matchmaking.py)
- Relevant MatchmakeExtension methods: BrowseMatchmakeSession (search criteria + range), AutoMatchmakeWithSearchCriteria_Postpone (list of search criteria + a gathering to create if none found), UpdateMatchmakeSessionAttribute (List<u32> attribs), ModifyCurrentGameAttribute (attribIndex, value). — [NintendoClients wiki: Matchmake Extension Protocol](https://github.com/kinnay/NintendoClients/wiki/Matchmake-Extension-Protocol)
- Pia (P2P layer): matchmaking via NEX, then direct connections into a "mesh" whose host accepts or rejects join requests. Packets are encrypted with a session key that, in NEX mode (Pia up to 5.45), "is obtained from the game server during matchmaking". — [NintendoClients wiki: Pia Overview](https://github.com/kinnay/NintendoClients/wiki/Pia-Overview); [NintendoClients wiki: Pia Protocol](https://github.com/kinnay/NintendoClients/wiki/Pia-Protocol)
- HDR precedent for isolation: disable Quickplay in the mod and rely on arenas. — [blu-dev gist](https://gist.github.com/blu-dev/e227d57a9e745fd8768bc3d41c8ea6b4)

### Inferences
- **Isolation lever 1, matchmaking field tagging (no server changes):** patch `cross_f.rpx` where Smash 4 builds its MatchmakeSession and search criteria. Put a modpack marker in `game_mode` (for example, high bits set) or in an `attribs[i]` index that vanilla always sends as an exact or ranged criterion. Pretendo's filter is exact or ranged, so modded sessions fall outside vanilla's criteria and vice versa. Caveat: if vanilla sends an empty criterion for a field, it matches anything, so vanilla could still join modded sessions through that field. The field must be one vanilla always constrains, and the modded client must constrain it too. Index 1 is ignored by Pretendo's filter (selection method), so don't use it. `codeword` doesn't exist at NEX 3.6. Which fields Smash 4 fills and searches on is undocumented. Pretendo's handler prints `anyGathering.FormatToString`, so a packet capture or a Pretendo dev log would show them.
- **Encode the version, not just "modded":** put a hash of the gameplay manifest (truncated to 16–24 bits) into the marker field, so different modpack versions also stop matching each other. This is the matchmaking-level equivalent of Dolphin's sync hash. With a growing pack this matters: v1.3 vs v1.4 is as fatal as modded vs vanilla.
- **Isolation lever 2, P2P-level guard:** add an RPX cave that exchanges the manifest hash in the first Pia reliable message (or in `m_ApplicationBuffer` / join message). On mismatch, refuse or leave before the match starts. This covers Friends/arena joins that bypass search criteria.
- **Isolation lever 3, a separate game server (needs Pretendo cooperation or self-hosting):** a different access key and game server would need a separate server instance. Pretendo's server is AGPL Go and can be self-hosted, but Wii U clients would then need redirecting (inference; not verified for Smash 4).
- **Policy:** Pretendo's own rules make "modded players only meet consenting modded players" a requirement in practice. Field tagging plus Friends-only/arena play matches the "private matches where everyone knows" allowance. Matching vanilla strangers in For Glory with gameplay mods would be cheating under the FAQ and risks console bans.
- **Distribution for a growing pack:**
  - Ship one canonical package with a manifest (file path to SHA-256) covering every gameplay-relevant file: `cross_f.rpx` or the RPX patch list, param, ACMD, MSC, motion, LVD, stage and fighter folders, plus new kind tables.
  - Have the launcher or updater verify it at start (Slippi's model).
  - Show the manifest hash (or short version code) in-game, and put it in the matchmaking tag.
  - Keep cosmetic files out of the hash so skins and music can stay optional (Forge client-only-mod model).
  - Keep a stable channel and a nightly channel, as HDR does, and make sure the two cannot match each other.
- **New fighter kinds and stages:** a vanilla peer cannot represent kinds 0x42 or 0x43 or new stage IDs. Because of this, the isolation has to be hard (matchmaking plus handshake), not a warning.
